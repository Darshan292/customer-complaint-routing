1. Document Control and Governance
This procedure defines the controlled operating model for receiving, classifying, prioritizing, routing, assigning, resolving and
closing customer complaints through the Enterprise Complaint Routing platform. It is intended for operational use by customer
support, service desk, resolver teams, operations managers and platform administrators.

                                                                      changes

                                                                      incidents or audit findings

1.3 Procedure Map
      Intake → AI analysis → priority determination → SLA calculation → team routing → resolver selection → execution → verification →
       resolution → closure.
      Automatic assignment is the default path. Manual assignment is an authorized operational override when no eligible resolver is
       available or urgent intervention is required.
      IN_PROGRESS requires an assigned resolver. Assignment alone does not mean work has started.
    OPERATING PRINCIPLE
    The platform provides recommendations; the accountable operator remains responsible for the final operational decision in
    exceptions, high-impact cases and manual overrides.

operational impact. The intake interface may use free-text description plus optional customer-provided priority. The platform

2.2 AI Analysis
1.   Classify the ticket into category and subcategory.
2.   Extract customer intent, sentiment, emotion, business impact and operational keywords.
3.   Determine an AI priority score and recommended priority level.
4.   Calculate the SLA applicable to the final priority level and customer/service policy.
5.   Resolve the complaint to an operational team using the canonical team taxonomy.
6.   Evaluate the resolver workforce and assign the highest-scoring eligible resolver when possible.

 PRIORITY CONTROL
 Customer-selected priority is a signal, not an authorization to bypass policy. The final priority may be increased when security,
 business impact, customer tier, urgency or other approved signals indicate greater operational risk.

3. Team Routing and Resolver Assignment
3.1 Canonical Team Routing
The platform must route tickets against canonical operational teams rather than relying on free-text labels. Legacy or business-
facing names are normalized before workforce matching. Examples include Finance Team → Finance Operations and General

3.2 Automatic Assignment
7. The assignment engine evaluates normal-workforce candidates in the ticket’s operational team.
8. The engine selects the highest-scoring eligible resolver and stores the assignment score and explanation.
9. The ticket remains OPEN after assignment until the resolver explicitly starts work.
10. The system creates an assignment event and notifies the customer and resolver according to the notification policy.
3.3 Manual Assignment
When automatic assignment returns no eligible resolver, the ticket remains visible in the unassigned queue. An authorized
administrator may manually assign the ticket to an active resolver in the correct operational team. Manual assignment may
override capacity restrictions, but the interface must display an explicit capacity-override warning and record the decision in
the audit trail.

 NO SILENT FAILURE
 A bulk assignment operation must return an actionable reason for every skipped ticket, including the resolved operational team and
 whether the constraint was workforce availability, capacity, skill fit, or another policy rule.

4. Resolver Work Procedure
4.1 Queue Review
Resolvers must begin each work period by reviewing their queue, current SLA exposure and workload. Tickets should be
worked in order of operational risk, considering SLA breach/at-risk state, priority and business impact rather than creation

4.2 AI Resolution Assistant
For suitable tickets, the resolver may request an AI-generated resolution recommendation. The assistant should use the ticket
context, approved knowledge sources and historical cases where available. AI guidance is advisory and must be validated by
the resolver before execution.
 Use the recommendation as an investigation aid, not as an authorization to alter production systems.
 Prefer approved knowledge-base procedures and known successful historical resolutions.
 Do not represent an AI suggestion as an observed fact or confirmed remediation.
 Escalate when the recommendation indicates security, data-loss, major outage or other high-risk intervention.
4.3 Similar Resolved Cases
Before performing novel troubleshooting, review similar tickets that were successfully resolved. Historical cases should be used
to understand proven diagnostic paths, known symptoms, effective remediation and lessons learned. Similarity is evidence for
investigation, not proof that the same cause exists.

 RESOLVER STANDARD
 Every resolved ticket should leave enough structured and narrative evidence that another resolver can understand the issue,
 reproduce the investigation path and recognize why the resolution was considered successful.

5. SLA Monitoring and Escalation
5.1 SLA Control
SLA commitments are driven by the final priority and applicable business calendar. The platform tracks first-response and
resolution commitments, including pause/resume behavior where policy permits. The same SLA service must be used by the
dashboard, ticket detail, resolver queue and escalation process so that displayed states remain consistent.
 State                                        Meaning                                        Required response


 ON_TRACK                                     Sufficient time remains against the current    Continue normal execution while monitoring
                                              commitment.                                    queue risk.


 AT_RISK                                      The ticket is approaching the SLA threshold.   Prioritize work, re-evaluate workload and
                                                                                             consider escalation.


 BREACHED                                     The committed deadline has passed.             Immediate operational attention; escalate
                                                                                             according to priority and ownership policy.


 NO_SLA                                       No applicable SLA was established.             Follow service-specific exception handling and
                                                                                             confirm configuration.




5.2 Escalation Triggers
 Critical priority requiring elevated operational attention.
 First-response SLA breach.
 Resolution SLA breach.
 Repeated rework or customer dissatisfaction indicating a service-management concern.
 Security, privacy, financial or regulatory exposure beyond the resolver’s authority.
 Capacity or workforce conditions that prevent timely completion.
5.3 Escalation Procedure
11. Confirm the trigger and capture the factual reason.
12. Set the appropriate escalation level and operational owner.
13. Notify the resolver, customer when appropriate, and designated operations owner.
14. Continue ticket execution unless ownership is formally transferred.
15. Record escalation decision, reason, time and outcome in the audit trail.
 ESCALATION DISCIPLINE
 Escalation is not a substitute for ownership. The ticket must always have a clear current owner or an explicit queue state with a
 named operational function responsible for the next action.

6.2 Audit Trail Requirements
Audit data should answer four questions for every material action: who performed it, what changed, when it changed, and
why. The ticket timeline should provide an operator-friendly representation; the underlying audit log should preserve
structured event data for investigation and compliance.
 Assignment events must preserve previous and new resolver where applicable.
 Manual overrides must identify the actor and justification.
 Priority changes must preserve the previous and new priority and the actor type (customer, resolver or admin).
 Status changes must preserve the state transition and timestamp.
 Escalations must preserve level, owner, reason and time.
6.3 Customer Communication Standard
Customer-facing messages must be concise, factual and action-oriented. They should explain what the support organization has
done, what is needed from the customer, and what happens next. Avoid exposing internal scoring, resolver workload, internal
notes, security controls or other confidential operational information.

 QUALITY CHECK
 Before closing a ticket, confirm that the customer-facing explanation matches the actual recorded resolution and that no internal-
 only information has been placed in a public comment.
